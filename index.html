Part one

Asking is not yet directing

A chat answers your question and stops. An agent takes a goal you set, works within tools and permissions you approve, shows its evidence, and waits for your decision. The weekend is about that second thing, kept firmly in your hands.

When you ask AI a question

  • You type a question and read a reply.
  • You copy, paste, and redo it yourself.
  • Nothing is checked unless you check it.
  • There is no plan, and no place to stop it.

When you direct an agent

  • You set a goal with real constraints.
  • It proposes a plan you can read first.
  • It uses only the tools you allow.
  • It shows evidence, then waits for your approval.

Part five

Anatomy of a bounded personal agent

One picture of the trust boundary the whole weekend teaches: what you direct, where the agent works, and what it may never do without you.

Read this map in 30 seconds

  1. You set the destination.
  2. The agent proposes and works within approved resources.
  3. Evidence and permissions constrain the route.
  4. You approve, correct, or stop.
Memory
information you deliberately allow the assistant to keep for later.
Permissions
what you have allowed it to use or reach.
Stopping condition
the point where it pauses instead of continuing on its own.

  1. Your direction

    You hold the goal, the context, the constraints, and the desired output. Everything starts here.

  2. Agent workspace

    Inside your direction, the agent keeps a plan, temporary context, only the tools you approved, and only the durable memory you approved.

  3. Trust boundary

    Around the workspace sit permissions, the evidence check, the stopping condition, and your approval. No action reaches beyond this boundary without your approval.

  4. The useful artifact

    The result sits outside the boundary. It is an output you approved, never proof that the agent acted on its own.

  • The evidence check can send the plan back for another pass.
  • Your correction updates the durable memory the agent is allowed to keep.
